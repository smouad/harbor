A safe harbor for your tabs.

Harbor is a free, open-source browser for the Mac, written in Swift on WebKit. Your tabs live in a calm sidebar, and every space keeps its own logins.

Download for Mac Follow the developer on GitHub

Every tab in one column, with room for its title.

Tabs run down the side of the window, so a long title stays a title. The sites you open every day sit in a grid above them, and pinned tabs come back to their home page.

Switch spaces. Each one keeps its own tabs, logins and cookies.

  • AddressType a site or a search.
  • FavoritesThe sites you open every day, one click away.
  • Pinned tabsThey stay put and remember their home page.
  • Open tabsTitles get the whole width of the column.

Work and home, signed in separately.

Each space has its own color, its own tabs, and its own logins and cookies. Sign in to the same site in two spaces and the two sessions never meet.

Idle tabs let go of memory. The ones you need stay loaded.

Tabs you have left alone unload on their own, and Harbor hands memory back when your Mac runs short. Pinned tabs, anything playing audio and calls stay loaded.

First launch steps

This preview is not notarized yet, so macOS stops it the first time you open it. Letting it through takes three steps.

  1. Open the disk image and drag Harbor into Applications.
  2. Open Harbor. macOS blocks it and shows a warning; close the warning.
  3. Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway.
